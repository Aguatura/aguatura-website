Main Challenges

Global agricultural systems face mounting pressures from climate disruption, resource depletion, and rapid population growth, threatening both food security and local ecosystems.

  • Rising global temperatures, prolonged droughts, and severe weather events disrupting traditional crop yields.
  • High freshwater consumption and reliance on synthetic inputs in soil-based farming practices.
  • Widespread soil degradation and loss of fertile arable land available for conventional cultivation.
  • Growing global population demanding higher food production under increasingly constrained resources.
  • Lack of hands-on environmental education in schools regarding circular economy and nature-based solutions.

Key Facts

Over 90%

Aquaponic systems can reduce water consumption by up to 90% compared with conventional soil-based agriculture thanks to water recirculation.

Source: FAO, 2018
150+ species

More than 150 species of vegetables, herbs, flowers, and small trees have been successfully grown in aquaponic systems worldwide.

Source: FAO, 2021
72%

Agriculture accounts for approximately 72% of global freshwater withdrawals, making water-efficient farming systems such as aquaponics increasingly important.

Source: FAO, 2025

Our Approach

Fish & Seeds was developed by five EDHEC Business School students to introduce aquaponics to primary school children in Roubaix. Through interactive workshops, partnerships with local schools, and media outreach, the project fosters curiosity and environmental responsibility from an early age.

Educational Workshops

Interactive sessions with primary school students in Roubaix, encouraging hands-on exploration.

Community Partnerships

Close collaboration with local primary schools, educators, and community actors like Echo Village.

Field Engagement

Direct practical experience, demonstrating real-world agricultural setups to young students.

Living Systems & Loops

Teaching the core biological mechanisms of aquaponics as a closed-loop ecosystem.

Learning from Living Systems

Water Cycles

  • Aquaponics begins with water as the central resource. Instead of being used once and wasted, water circulates through the system, connecting fish, plants, and natural filtration processes.

Resource Awareness

  • Fish & Seeds encourages a more conscious view of food production. It highlights how much water, space, and external input conventional agriculture often requires.

Future Thinking

  • Beyond growing food, the project is a learning space for imagining more resilient food systems inspired by nature-based principles.

Natural Balance

  • The project shows how different living elements support each other: fish provide nutrients, plants clean the water, and beneficial bacteria transform waste.

How can You take Action?

Micro Level

  • Set up a small classroom or home aquaponics kit to observe ecosystem loops.
  • Participate in workshops on water conservation and circular farming practices.
  • Encourage peer-to-peer learning by sharing eco-projects with family and friends.

Meso Level

  • Partner local municipalities with schools to fund urban agriculture initiatives.
  • Integrate experiential environmental learning into official school curricula.
  • Develop neighborhood educational gardens to boost local ecological awareness.
