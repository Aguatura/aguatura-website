The Challenge

Agriculture is increasingly challenged by climate change, resource scarcity, and growing food demand. Rising temperatures, prolonged droughts, extreme weather events, and soil degradation are placing significant pressure on conventional farming systems worldwide.

At the same time, a growing global population requires more food to be produced with fewer available natural resources. Agriculture also remains heavily dependent on freshwater and chemical inputs, raising concerns about environmental impacts and long-term sustainability. These challenges highlight the need for more resilient and resource-efficient approaches to food production.

Our project

Fish & Seeds was developed by five first-year students at EDHEC Business School to introduce aquaponics to primary school children in Roubaix. Through a series of interactive workshops, the project encouraged children to explore sustainable food production and innovative agricultural techniques in a fun and practical way.

The main objective was to inspire environmental awareness from an early age. As the decision-makers of tomorrow, children play a key role in building a more sustainable future. By introducing them to aquaponics, the project sought to foster curiosity, environmental responsibility, and a lasting interest in sustainable development.

To maximize the impact of the project, we established partnerships with several primary schools in Roubaix and worked closely with teachers to organize educational workshops on aquaponics and sustainable agriculture. We also promoted the initiative through local media to raise broader community awareness.

Learning from Living Systems

Water Cycles

Aquaponics begins with water as the central resource. Instead of being used once and wasted, water circulates through the system, connecting fish, plants, and natural filtration processes.

Resource Awareness

Fish & Seeds encourages a more conscious view of food production. It highlights how much water, space, and external input conventional agriculture often requires.

Future Thinking

Beyond growing food, the project is a learning space for imagining more resilient food systems inspired by nature-based principles.

Natural Balance

The project shows how different living elements support each other: fish provide nutrients, plants clean the water, and beneficial bacteria transform waste.
