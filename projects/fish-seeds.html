Main Challenges in Environmental Literacy

While environmental challenges are becoming more urgent, traditional education often lacks practical ways to help young people understand natural systems and feel empowered to act.

  • Growing disconnection between young people and natural ecosystems in urban environments.
  • Theoretical teaching methods that fail to make abstract environmental principles tangible.
  • Limited opportunities for students to experience closed-loop systems and sustainable solutions firsthand.
  • Need for inspiring educational models that foster early environmental stewardship and confidence.
  • Gap between classroom learning and practical eco-initiatives within local communities.

Fish & Seeds Approach

Fish & Seeds was developed by five EDHEC Business School students to make environmental literacy tangible for primary school children in Roubaix. By using living aquaponics setups as an interactive learning model, the project connects classroom concepts with real-world ecological stewardship.

Educational Workshops

Interactive sessions with primary school students, encouraging curiosity and hands-on discovery.

Community Partnerships

Collaborating closely with local schools, teachers, and eco-actors like Echo Village.

Field Engagement

Giving students practical experience with living setups to understand real-world eco-solutions.

Living Systems & Loops

Using aquaponics to demonstrate natural balance, nutrient cycles, and water conservation.

What Students Learn from Living Systems

Water & Resource Cycles

  • Students observe how water circulates and connects living organisms, showing that resources can be saved and reused continuously rather than wasted.

Ecological Interconnectedness

  • The project illustrates how plants, fish, and microorganisms rely on one another, teaching children that every part of a natural ecosystem has a role.

Systems & Future Thinking

  • By exploring closed loops, students learn to think in systems—a mindset that helps them imagine more sustainable solutions for their own communities.

Action-Oriented Mindset

  • Hands-on experiences build confidence, turning abstract environmental concern into tangible daily habits and creative sustainability ideas.

How Can You Take Action?

Individual & Everyday Action

  • Learn about circular economy principles and share practical eco-tips with family and classmates.
  • Start small sustainability experiments at home, such as composting or window-sill herb gardens.
  • Reduce daily food waste and support local, sustainably produced food initiatives.

Community & School Level

  • Organize environmental awareness workshops or field trips in your local school or community center.
  • Pitch and implement sustainable ideas on campus, like waste-sorting programs or green spaces.
  • Connect with student eco-clubs to turn sustainability concepts into collaborative community projects.
