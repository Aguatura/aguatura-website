Main Challenges

Global climate initiatives face mounting pressures from escalating emissions, resource depletion, and fragmented policies, threatening global ecosystems and urban resilience.

  • Severe coastal flooding and storm surges threatening the NYC metropolitan area due to rising sea levels and extreme precipitation.
  • Climate change as a meta-risk: acting as a threat multiplier that triggers cascading failures across transportation, energy, and urban infrastructure.
  • Growing freshwater scarcity and degradation of critical natural water resources worldwide.
  • Disconnect between high-level policy commitments and grassroots community action.

Our Approach

Our participation in Climate Week NYC showed us how important it is to connect global discussions with local action. Through exchanges with experts, researchers, NGOs, and community leaders from around the world, we gained new perspectives on climate resilience and water sustainability. These conversations strengthened our commitment to promoting dialogue, sharing knowledge, and working closely with local communities. We believe that global ideas create the greatest impact when they are translated into meaningful action on the ground.

The Movie Screening

Special documentary screening narrated by Benedict Cumberbatch, raising awareness on human connection with nature and aquatic ecosystems.

Community Restoration

Hands-on environmental action partnering with the LES Ecological Center to actively restore local urban green spaces and foster grassroots ecological stewardship.

WaterWays Initiative

Engaging sessions uniting scientific insight and artistic expression to promote deep water literacy, watershed protection, and long-term conservation.

Columbia Pitch

Academic innovation grant awarded to the winning student project: the Circular Marketplace, a dedicated platform promoting college student resource-sharing and second-hand furniture reuse.

How can you take Action?

Micro Level

  • Participating in local restoration and conservation initiatives.
  • Reducing water and energy consumption through sustainable daily practices.
  • Engaging in workshops, educational programs, and community dialogues on climate and water challenges.

Meso Level

  • Developing partnerships between NGOs, universities, local authorities, and community groups.
  • Organizing webinars, conferences, and knowledge-sharing events.
  • Supporting research and innovation on water and climate resilience.
  • Turning global discussions, such as those held during NY Climate Week, into concrete projects adapted to local realities.
