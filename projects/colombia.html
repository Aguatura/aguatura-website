Main Challenges

Colombia hosts nearly 10% of global biodiversity on less than 1% of the Earth's land surface. However, rapid land-use changes and environmental pressures threaten critical natural ecosystems across the country.

  • Accelerating deforestation and illegal logging threatening high-biodiversity biomes.
  • Vulnerability of high-altitude páramo ecosystems supplying water to major urban hubs.
  • Pressure from unsustainable agricultural expansion and illegal mining activities.
  • Fragmented resource mobilization between international funders and local communities.

Our Approach

Partner

Together with WWF Colombia, regional authorities, and local fincas, our comprehensive approach focuses on bridging strategic institutional fundraising with hands-on ecosystem fieldwork, multi-stakeholder coalitions, and long-term community stewardship.

NGO Strategic Alignment

Effectively connecting international non-profit fundraising initiatives in Bogotá directly with high-impact, science-based biodiversity field programs across Colombia's unique regional biomes.

Páramo & Water Protection

Actively safeguarding high-altitude natural sponges and glacial ecosystems within Chingaza National Park to guarantee clean freshwater supplies and protect vulnerable endemic species.

Multi-Stakeholder Coalitions

Building strong cooperative networks and alliances under frameworks like "Unidos por el Planeta" to align environmental goals among NGOs, civil society groups, and local governments.

Sustainable Agroforestry

Integrating regenerative practices such as organic shade-grown coffee cultivation and native stingless bee meliponiculture to support biodiversity and secure sustainable local livelihoods.
