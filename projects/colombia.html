The Challenge

Colombia is one of the most biodiverse countries in the world, hosting nearly 10% of the planet's biodiversity despite covering less than 1% of the Earth's land surface.

However, its ecosystems are under growing pressure from deforestation, illegal mining, agricultural expansion, and climate change, putting many species and water reserves at risk.

Bogotá: NGO Fundraising & Strategic Alignment

Based in Bogotá, our engagement kicked off with strategic discussions on non-profit fundraising, resource mobilization, and scaling conservation impact across national programs.

Understanding how institutional funding connects to grassroots fieldwork is essential for ensuring long-term financial stability for environmental protection initiatives.

Medellín & Antioquia: Unidos por el Planeta

In Antioquia, WWF Colombia drives the initiative "Unidos por el Planeta". This strategic approach unites diverse actors from government agencies, civil society, academic institutions, and local communities.

By combining the unique strengths and resources of each sector, the initiative co-creates robust, long-term climate adaptation strategies to protect critical areas.

Chingaza National Park: Water & Biodiversity

Alongside WWF Colombia biologists, we explored Chingaza National Natural Park. Its high-altitude páramo ecosystem acts as a natural sponge, capturing and supplying nearly 80% of Bogotá's drinking water.

Protecting Chingaza is vital for endemic species like the spectacled bear, while securing long-term water security against climate-induced droughts.

Salento: Sustainable Agriculture & Coffee

In Salento, we combined a visit to a sustainable coffee finca with a meliponiculture course focusing on native stingless bees and their vital ecological role as pollinators.

This hands-on experience highlighted how agroforestry and fair trade support for small-scale producers safeguard both biodiversity and rural livelihoods.
