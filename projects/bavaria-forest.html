The Challenge

Forests around the world are already under significant pressure from climate change and environmental pollution. Rising temperatures, prolonged droughts, and increasing disturbances are weakening forest ecosystems and threatening their long-term resilience.

In my home region of Bavaria, one of the main challenges is rooted in the way forests were managed in the past. During the 1950s to 1970s, large areas were deliberately designed as monocultures, dominated by a few fast-growing and commercially valuable tree species such as spruce, pine, and larch.

While these forests performed rather well under past climate conditions, they have become highly vulnerable today. The lack of species diversity reduces their ability to adapt to changing environmental conditions. As a result, forests are increasingly affected by extreme droughts and bark beetle infestations.

Our Project & Restoration Approach

Together with his father, Johannes has been working for several years to gradually transform “sick” forests into more resilient and biodiverse ecosystems. This is a long-term process that requires patience, continuous learning, and close observation of nature.

Our approach focuses on increasing diversity and strengthening the forest’s ability to adapt to future climate conditions. One key measure is the gradual replacement of vulnerable monoculture species with a wider mix of tree species, introducing native and carefully selected non-native species better adapted to heat and drought.

Another important aspect is allowing natural processes to take place. Leaving dead trees and plant material intentionally in the forest provides habitats and nesting opportunities for birds, insects, and wildlife while regenerating the soil.

Key steps of our forest restoration project

1. Assess

Together with local forestry experts, we assessed the condition of our forest, identified key challenges, and established a baseline for future restoration measures.

2. Plan

Based on the assessment results, we defined long-term restoration measures, developed an action plan, and secured funding through relevant support programs.

3. Implement

We are gradually implementing restoration activities, including planting climate-resilient trees, enhancing biodiversity habitats, and improving forest resilience.

4. Monitor

Our forest is continuously monitored to evaluate progress, maintain implemented measures, and adapt management practices where improvements are needed.

How other forest owners can take action

Transforming forests toward climate resilience is a task that many private and public forest owners face. If you own forest land and want to make a positive impact, here are practical steps you can take:

  • Consult Local Forestry Authorities: Contact your regional forestry office (e.g., *Ämter für Ernährung, Landwirtschaft und Forsten* in Bavaria) for professional advice and tailored management plans.
  • Utilize State Funding & Subsidies: Take advantage of government grants and funding programs designed to support climate-resilient forest conversion and afforestation.
  • Engage with Forest Owner Associations: Join local *Forstbetriebsgemeinschaften* (FBG) to share experiences, coordinate timber harvesting, and learn from peer landowners.
  • Promote Natural Regeneration: Allow natural succession and keep deadwood where appropriate to build healthy soil and protect local biodiversity.
