Main Challenges

Forests around the world are already under significant pressure from climate change and environmental pollution. Rising temperatures, prolonged droughts, and increasing disturbances are weakening forest ecosystems and threatening their long-term resilience.

  • Rising temperatures and prolonged seasonal droughts threatening forest health.
  • Severe bark beetle outbreaks taking advantage of stressed monoculture spruce stands.
  • Historical legacy of 1950s–1970s single-species conifer plantations lacking genetic diversity.
  • High vulnerability to windthrow, soil compaction, and extreme weather disturbances.

Our Approach

Together with local experts and hands-on family management, our approach focuses on systematic restoration, species diversification, and building climate resilience from the ground up.

Monoculture Conversion

Gradually replacing vulnerable single-species conifer stands with a balanced, resilient mix of native and heat-tolerant tree species.

Climate Adaptation

Implementing targeted measures to mitigate drought stress, reduce bark beetle vulnerability, and protect forest health against extreme climate events.

Self-Sufficient Harvest

Combining sustainable self-sufficient harvesting practices with long-term forest conservation and continuous canopy protection.

Key steps of our forest restoration project

1. Assess

  • Together with local forestry experts, assess forest health condition and identify primary environmental vulnerabilities.

2. Plan

  • Define long-term restoration schedules, establish actionable adaptation targets, and secure regional support programs.

3. Implement

  • Plant climate-resilient mixed tree species, promote natural succession, and preserve beneficial deadwood habitats.

4. Monitor

  • Continuously evaluate forest growth progression, maintain protective measures, and adapt stewardship practices.

How can you take action

Micro Level

  • Consult Local Forestry Authorities: Contact regional forestry offices for professional advice and tailored management plans.
  • Promote Natural Regeneration: Allow natural succession and preserve deadwood on private land plots to enrich soil biology.
  • Practice Selective Harvesting: Balance self-sufficient wood harvesting carefully with continuous canopy cover protection.

Meso Level

  • Engage with Forest Owner Associations: Join local *Forstbetriebsgemeinschaften* (FBG) to coordinate regional timber harvesting and share peer expertise.
  • Utilize State Subsidies: Leverage municipal and regional government grants designed to support climate-resilient forest conversion.
  • Community Outreach & Education: Organize regional workshops and knowledge-sharing sessions on biodiversity preservation.
