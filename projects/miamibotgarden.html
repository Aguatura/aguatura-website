Main Challenges

South Florida's subtropical ecosystems face severe risks from rapid urban growth, habitat loss, and climate pressures.

  • Rapid loss of native tropical plants due to invasive species and urban sprawl.
  • Sea-level rise and saltwater intrusion threatening freshwater and wetlands.
  • More frequent extreme weather events and hurricanes impacting collections.
  • Erosion and changing water cycles affecting regional watershed stability.
  • Need for stronger public engagement and science education for biodiversity.

Fairchild Approach

Combining world-class horticulture, advanced science, NASA space partnerships, the Million Orchid Project, and community education, Fairchild leads biodiversity preservation.

NASA Partnership: Botanical Labs & Space Agriculture

Partnering directly with NASA, Fairchild operates specialized labs to test plants for space missions. Using custom chambers, tissue culture, and 360-degree gravity simulators, scientists study plant responses to microgravity.

The Million Orchid Project: Urban Restoration & Citizen Science

A pioneering urban ecology initiative to reintroduce millions of native orchids into Miami's public trees. Through sterile plant culture taught in schools, students and citizens help restore urban biodiversity.

Rare Plant Conservation

Propagating wild-collected species, managing seed banks, and protecting plants against invasive threats like iguanas.

Art & Science Collaborations

Uniting artists and scientists through sculpture and endangered species print shows to boost environmental literacy.

Climate Resilience & Restoration

Using adaptive management for fire-adapted habitats, ecological corridors, and urban cooling strategies.

Field Challenges & Management

Subtropical ecosystems face heavy pressures. Managers often struggle with budget limits rather than lack of intent.

"If habitats are mismanaged, it's due to a lack of funds, not intent. Managers need clear data to track invasive plants."

Community Stewardship

Regional meetings involving local tribes help blend cultural fire traditions with modern conservation needs.

"When we bring communities and local tribes together, we bridge the gap. Education and collaboration are key."

Urban Greening

Using tissue culture and school outreach to bring native orchids back into city trees.

"Restoration never stops. We started with schools, giving students sterile plant material to become active stewards."

Genomics & Big Data

Advanced sequencers track billions of base pairs to provide precise genetic mapping for fragile species.

"Modern sequencers process three billion pairs, giving us exact genetic maps to protect fragile species."

How to Take Action

Micro Level

  • Plant native species and pollinator-friendly flowers in your garden or balcony.
  • Conserve water and reduce chemical fertilizers around your home.
  • Support botanical education and join local conservation workshops.

Meso Level

  • Join citizen science programs like the Million Orchid Project.
  • Partner with local conservation groups and community gardens.
  • Advocate for urban tree canopy protection and sustainable policies.
