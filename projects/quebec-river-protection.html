Project Location (Québec)

Main Challenges

Unlike centralized administrative models like in France, Québec relies heavily on municipal decentralization, making cross-collaboration essential.

  • Preventing the construction of new large hydroelectric dams.
  • Reducing wastewater overflows into rivers.
  • Fighting watersheds privatization and improving public access to nature.
  • Helping municipalities adapt to climate-related flooding and extreme rainfall.
  • Engaging younger and more diverse communities in environmental action.

Fondation Rivières Projects & Approach

Acting as a vital bridge between grassroots citizens, scientific data, and policy decision-makers, Fondation Rivières operates right in the middle to advocate for sustainable water management.

Smart Microgrids Project

A project exploring community-based energy production through smart microgrids. The initiative promotes local renewable energy, energy efficiency, and alternatives to large-scale hydroelectric developments, while seeking to strengthen collaboration with Indigenous communities.

Rivière du Nord Pilot Project

An innovative pilot project bringing together municipalities across the Rivière du Nord watershed to improve water quality, identify pollution sources, and coordinate river management at the watershed scale rather than through fragmented municipal governance.

Alliance for Access to Nature

A coalition advocating for better public access to rivers, lakes, and natural areas. The initiative addresses shoreline privatization and promotes legislative changes to ensure equitable access to nature for all residents.

La Grande Descente

A large citizen mobilization event organized on the Rivière du Nord to raise awareness about river conservation, water quality, and public access to waterways.

Solutions Rivières

A climate adaptation project helping riverside communities address flooding and erosion through citizen workshops, nature-based solutions, and river restoration approaches that allow rivers more space to function naturally.

Key Steps for River Protection: How Local Communities Can Take Action

1. Prevent new hydroelectric dams

  • Promote alternative renewable energy solutions such as community-scale solar and wind power.
  • Support Indigenous communities in protecting rivers from future dam projects.

2. Reduce wastewater pollution

  • Upgrade aging water and sewage infrastructure.
  • Improve monitoring of wastewater treatment facilities.
  • Encourage municipal cooperation to address overflow events.

3. Restore natural river functions

  • Allow rivers more space to flow naturally.
  • Avoid excessive shoreline hardening with rocks and concrete.
  • Implement nature-based solutions to reduce erosion and flood risks.

4. Protect public access to rivers

  • Oppose excessive privatization of shorelines.
  • Create legal and political mechanisms guaranteeing public access to lakes and rivers.
  • Support local campaigns for accessible natural spaces.

5. Strengthen citizen engagement

  • Educate residents about flood risks and river ecosystems.
  • Support citizen groups with scientific, legal, and communication expertise.
  • Build partnerships between communities, municipalities, and policymakers.
