Project Location (Québec)

Grassroots Conservation & Policy Reform in Québec

During our North American exchange in 2026, the Aguatura team analyzed local water conservation models in Québec. The province faces unique ecological challenges: while hydro-electricity historically defined Québec's energy identity, distant mega-dams often impacted Indigenous territories and flooded massive natural basins.

Unlike centralized administrative models in Europe, Québec relies heavily on municipal decentralization without always providing local authorities with the necessary tools, funds, or coordination mechanisms. Environmental organizations act as vital bridges between citizen movements, scientific data, and government decision-makers.

Core Focus Areas & Solutions

Decentralized Energy & Micro-Hydro

Pushing back against destructive mega-dams and large-scale infrastructure projects that flood indigenous lands. By promoting local micro-hydro and energy decentralization, communities gain higher energy consciousness and resilience against grid failures during severe winter ice storms.

Municipal Wastewater Tracking

Collecting and visualizing municipal wastewater performance data through transparent Power BI tools. The annual rankings hold local politicians accountable, shifting the focus from pointing fingers to collaborative municipal capacity-building.

Shoreline Access & Room for Rivers

Challenging widespread privatization of riverbanks ("accès aux berges") and advocating for legislative reform. Concurrently, promoting "espaces de liberté" (room for rivers) to let natural waterways follow their course rather than blocking them with expensive rock walls.
