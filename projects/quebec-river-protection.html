Main Challenges

Québec's watershed ecosystems face severe structural and environmental hurdles due to fragmented municipal governance and growing climate pressures.

  • Proliferation of new large hydroelectric dam projects threatening pristine river ecosystems.
  • Persistent wastewater overflows contaminating local rivers and aquatic habitats.
  • Massive privatization of lake and river shorelines cutting off public access to nature.
  • Vulnerability of riverside communities to extreme rainfall and climate-induced flooding.
  • Low engagement and disconnection of younger and diverse communities from environmental protection.

Fondation Rivières Approach

Acting as a vital bridge between grassroots citizens, scientific data, and policy decision-makers, Fondation Rivières operates right in the middle to advocate for sustainable water management.

Smart Microgrids Project

A project exploring community-based energy production through smart microgrids. The initiative promotes local renewable energy, energy efficiency, and alternatives to large-scale hydroelectric developments, while opposing new hydro-dams and conventional hydro-energy.

Rivière du Nord Pilot Project

An innovative pilot project bringing together municipalities across the Rivière du Nord watershed to improve water quality, identify pollution sources, and coordinate river management at the watershed scale rather than through fragmented municipal governance.

Alliance for Access to Nature

A coalition advocating for better public access to rivers, lakes, and natural areas. The initiative addresses shoreline privatization and promotes legislative changes to ensure equitable access to nature for all residents.

La Grande Descente

A large citizen mobilization event organized on the Rivière du Nord to raise awareness about river conservation, water quality, and public access to waterways.

Solutions Rivières

A climate adaptation project helping riverside communities address flooding and erosion through citizen workshops, nature-based solutions, and river restoration approaches that allow rivers more space to function naturally.

Experts Insights

Fondation Rivières Team

André Bélanger (Executive Director) & David Beauchamp (Communications)

Meeting in the heart of Montreal on the drivers of environmental change and water governance.

For over twenty years, the organization has worked for the protection of waterways and more equitable access to nature. The major challenge is no longer just observing the issues, but bringing citizens, municipalities, and decision-makers together.

Water Governance and Climate Change

Extreme weather events are accelerating and overloading wastewater management infrastructure. Often isolated, municipalities lack the tools to cooperate at the natural scale of watersheds.

"Now, an equivalent of a month's rain falls in a single day. Sewer systems are not adapted. Things are changing too fast compared to our adaptation capacities."

Decentralization and Energy Perception

Historical large-scale projects far from consumption centers have disconnected citizens from their real energy footprint. Promoting micro-grids helps rebuild a cultural bond and proximity.

"Bringing energy production closer to citizens makes people much more sensitive to their energy consumption."

Public Access to Shorelines

Despite abundant bodies of water, massive shoreline privatization creates deep inequalities in access to nature, restricting natural spaces mostly to privileged groups.

"Here, 98% of shorelines are inaccessible. Everything is privatized. Something public is entirely inaccessible."

From the Ground to Public Policy

By combining strategy, communication, and technical expertise, Fondation Rivières plays an essential bridging role to anchor sustainable change where problems are experienced.

"The link between citizens, field mobilization, and the political world—I think that is the key position we hold."

How can You take Action?

Micro Level

  • Avoid pouring oils, chemicals, or harsh household products down the drain to protect local waterways.
  • Monitor and reduce daily water consumption at home.
  • Support local public awareness initiatives and share water conservation tips.

Meso Level

  • Adopt a river through Fondation Rivières to help protect and monitor its ecosystem.
  • Join local mobilization events and watershed committees.
  • Engage with municipal authorities to advocate for better wastewater management and public access.
