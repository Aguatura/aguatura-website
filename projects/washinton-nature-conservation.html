Evolving from Historic Roots to Inclusive Action

During our 2026 field exchange in North America, the Aguatura team sat down with Alison Pearce, Ph.D., Executive Director of Nature Forward (formerly known as the Audubon Naturalist Society). Founded in 1897, it is the Capital Region's oldest environmental organization.

Having served for over a decade in key roles—including Restoration Director and Deputy Director—Alison Pearce now leads the organization in its new chapter. Nature Forward focuses heavily on scalable community solutions, environmental education, stream restoration at its historic Woodend Sanctuary, and holding local and state governments accountable for environmental protection.

Field Insights & Interview Quotes

Alison Pearce, Ph.D.

Executive Director, Nature Forward (Washington D.C.)

Discussing organizational evolution, scalable nature solutions, equity, and community-driven environmental advocacy during our 2026 exchange.

On Scalable Solutions vs. Perfectionism:
"If you're looking for solutions, at some point you've got to realize that perfection is the enemy of progress. If a strategy relies on rare native plants that people can't buy in stores, it won't engage the masses. Real impact comes from strategies that can be adopted by everyone."
"Our purpose as an organization is not our organization—it's nature. We will never work ourselves out of business. Remembering that existing is not our sole purpose keeps us focused on driving real change in the world."
— Alison Pearce, Nature Forward 2026
On Modernizing Membership and Equity:
"We shifted our membership model. Instead of maintaining an older, exclusive structure with high paywalls, we introduced an accessible $20 entry point and partnered with community hubs like 'The Sanctuary' in D.C. to truly listen and build shared purpose."
On Stream Restoration and Community Ownership:
"Our restoration team doesn't just plant trees; they build community capacity. When children and neighbors help care for local rain gardens and streams, they build a lifelong identity connected with the natural world."

Core Focus Areas & Solutions

Inclusive Community Engagement

Lowering financial and social barriers to membership. Transitioning from traditional top-down models to active listening partnerships with diverse local communities and neighborhood hubs across the Capital Region.

Watershed & Stream Restoration

Managing stormwater runoff and restoring urban watersheds at Woodend Sanctuary and regional parks. Transforming technical engineering projects into hands-on educational opportunities for youth and volunteers.

Local Advocacy & Accountability

Pushing local county and state levels of government to enforce environmental regulations. Collaborating in strong coalitions to protect natural habitats against unchecked urban development and policy rollbacks.
